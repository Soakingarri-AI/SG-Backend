---
title: The Mali Empire
era: 1235–1600 CE
region: West Africa
---

The Mali Empire was a vast West African state founded by Sundiata Keita around
1235 CE after his victory at the Battle of Kirina. At its height under Mansa Musa
(reigned c. 1312–1337), Mali controlled the trans-Saharan gold and salt trade and
encompassed much of the western Sudan.

Mansa Musa's 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca became legendary: the gold his caravan
distributed in Cairo reportedly depressed its value in the region for years. Under
his patronage, Timbuktu grew into a renowned centre of Islamic scholarship, home to
the Sankore Madrasah and thousands of manuscripts.

The empire's wealth rested on control of the goldfields of Bambuk and Bure and on
taxation of the caravan trade in salt from the Sahara. Griots (jeliw) preserved the
oral history and genealogy of the ruling Keita lineage, and the Epic of Sundiata
remains a cornerstone of Mande cultural memory.
